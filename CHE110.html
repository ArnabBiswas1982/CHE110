ENVIRONMENTAL SCIENCE PROJECT

Construction Debris Affecting Urban Drainage & Land Use

Rapid urbanization and infrastructure expansion generate massive volumes of construction and demolition (C&D) waste—including crushed concrete, broken bricks, excavated soil, timber, and metallic scrap. When dumped along roadsides or left uncontained near stormwater inlets, this debris chokes urban drainage networks, triggers severe waterlogging during monsoons, degrades surrounding soil quality, and turns productive public land into informal waste yards. BUILDWISE is an interactive environmental monitoring and awareness platform designed to bridge classroom research with real-world civic action—enabling students, citizens, and faculty evaluators to study urban drainage impacts, explore sustainable material recovery, and log live geo-tagged field inspections.

EXPLORE THE ISSUE

Where Construction Meets the Environment

Every stage of urban development—from site excavation and structural renovation to full-scale demolition—interacts directly with the surrounding ecosystem. Without proper on-site segregation and containment berms, loose sediment and rubble wash into street gutters, reducing the hydraulic capacity of subterranean storm drains and accelerating urban flash floods. By combining environmental science principles with on-ground site documentation, this platform highlights how responsible waste sorting, recycled aggregate reuse, and buffer protection around drainage channels can transform how growing cities manage construction debris.

KEY INFORMATION

Why This Topic Matters

Urban growth creates pressure on land and infrastructure. Responsible debris management can help reduce avoidable environmental problems.

🧱

Waste Generation

Construction and demolition activities can produce large quantities of mixed solid waste.

💧

Drainage Blockage

Loose debris and sediment can enter drains and reduce the effective flow path for stormwater.

🌳

Land Pressure

Unmanaged waste can occupy useful land and create environmental concerns.
